How the calculations are made

Payback = investment / annual net benefit, where annual net benefit = annual gross benefit − annual operating cost.

NPV = −investment + annual net benefit × annuity factor.

ROI = (total gross benefit − total cost) / total cost; total cost = investment + annual operating cost × horizon.

Investment uses the documented total funding ceiling. The annuity factor uses the entered horizon and discount rate.

Overall Budget

The max budget is CAD 1,000,000,000. CAD 900,000,000 is assigned to project activities and work packages. CAD 100,000,000 is kept as a reserve controlled by the sponsor.

The first approved spending should be a maximum of CAD 10 million, this will include checking whether the sites and partners are suitable, reviewing privacy and other requirements, then preparing the purchases of the equipments like the sensors and compute services for the research software. (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, 2018; University of the Fraser Valley, Procurement Services, n.d.)

Resource planning - Procurement

Buyers, Suppliers and Release Conditions

Who Manages Purchasing?

UFV procurement service will manage the purchasing of all resources and the equipments, and they will collaborate with managers of the clinics and municipalities. Before purchasing anything UFV must check the budget and purchasing rules so that overspending can be avoided. (University of the Fraser Valley, Procurement Services, n.d.)

Computer services will be purchased after getting the consultation from the IT person and laboratory and filtration equipment ger the RFP from the suppliers first and then decide which one will be best suited for their research and budget.

For the selection of the supplier UFV procurement service can use the following metric.

  • 35 points: How well the product performs in user testing.
  • 25 points: Total cost over its lifetime.
  • 20 points: Installation and technical support.
  • 10 points: Accessibility.
  • 10 points: Training and knowledge transfer.

Return, Payback and the Decision to Expand

The project can track three types of benefits:

  1. Actual savings: Money that partners really save.
  2. Capacity improvements: More appointments or services available.
  3. Community benefits: Improvements to the wider community.
