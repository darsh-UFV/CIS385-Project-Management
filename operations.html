Project Stakeholders

HR Management and Responsibilities

The lab director will manage the research programme; manager of the clinic will review the alert and decide whether this alert is important or not. Research team of the lab will get the data, refine it and then feed it in the research software.

UFV Human Resources will decide employee job classifications. Confirm salaries and employment agreements.

Recruitment of Employees

  • First group: Project managers, privacy specialists, community relations staff and clinical reviewers.
  • Second group: Epidemiologists, forecasting specialists, health informatics specialists, software integration developers, accessibility specialists and quality assurance staff.
  • Third group: Trainers, technical support staff, research coordinators and evaluation staff.
  • Facilities and purchasing specialists will join when needed.

Students Can Help With

  • Testing the system using sample data.
  • Checking whether interfaces are easy to use.
  • Documenting system changes.

Communication With Stakeholders Include Sending Progress and Status Reports

Weekly forecast reports will be sent to clinic managers, project sponsor will get the weekly progress report and status report, and reports on the spending and unresolved issues. That communication channels will be used that are already being trusted by the stakeholders.

Conflicts And Handling Disagreements

Different communities may disagree about which clinic will receive the additional staff and supplies in priority the conflicts may happen, so to solve this preset rules should be distributed to the regional clinics, for example, remote clinics will get the priority or where visits are increasing at higher rate will get the extra staff first.

Sponsor will also receive the updated information on the unresolved issues and resolved ones.

Details

1 · Data access delayed

Trigger — No signed permission from the clinics

Proposed owner — Data collector

Prevention — get permissions early

2 · Privacy incident

Trigger — Suspected disclosure private information

Proposed owner — Privacy officer

Prevention — Review outputs before release and restrict access.

3 · False alert or missed rise

Trigger — Reviewer documents on error

Proposed owner — Clinical lead

Prevention — Independently compare signals with ordinary surveillance.

4 · Poor rural coverage

Trigger — A participating area loses a scheduled feed

Proposed owner — Epidemiology lead

Prevention — Publish completeness of coverage and make the report which will include what areas are covered

5 · Platform outage

Trigger — Scheduled briefing cannot be delivered

Proposed owner — IT service owner

Prevention — Rehearse restoration and alternate delivery.

6 · Reviewer fatigue

Trigger — Repeated actions and reviews overtime

Proposed owner — Operations manager

Prevention — Train the alternates and review hours weekly.

7 · Cost escalation

Trigger — Compliant bids exceed the work-package ceiling

Proposed owner — Procurement lead

Prevention — Compare whole-life pricing and phase commitments.

8 · Loss of trust

Trigger — Partner challenges an interpretation or requests withdrawal

Proposed owner — Engagement lead

Prevention — Agree use, review and correction rights in writing.

9 · Vendor dependence

Trigger — Supplier fails a contracted export or recovery test

Proposed owner — Commercial lead

Prevention — Require documented exports and transition assistance.

10 · Unfunded operation

Trigger — No signed operating authority at cutover review

Proposed owner — Executive sponsor

Prevention — Obtain a priced service plan and recurring funding decision.
